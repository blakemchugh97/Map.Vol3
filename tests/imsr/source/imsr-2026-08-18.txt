Three 15-member incident management teams and 196 fireline management personnel from Australia and 
New Zealand are assigned to support large fires in the Northwest and Great Basin geographic areas.  
 
One Large Airtanker and one Birddog from Manitoba, Canada have been deployed to Boise, ID in support of 
wildland fire operations. 
 
Two MAFFS C-130 airtankers and support personnel from the 302nd Airlift Wing (Air Force Reserve, CO) have 
been deployed to Klamath Falls, OR. Two MAFFS C-130 airtankers from the 146th Airlift Wing (CA Air National 
Guard) and respective support personnel have been deployed to Redding, CA to support wildland fire 
operations nationally. 
 
Two hundred forty soldiers from the 1st Battalion, 23rd Infantry Regiment, 7th Infantry Division based out of 
Joint Base Lewis McChord (JBLM) are deployed in support of wildland fire operations on the Modrite fire. An 
Incident Management Organization (IMO) is assigned to support the military contingent.

Predictive Services Discussion:  
 
Persistent very hot conditions are expected across southeast and south- central portions of the US where high 
temperatures will surge into the upper 90s to mid 100s.  This will coincide with RHs in the teens and afternoon 
wind gusts of 20-30 mph resulting in high-risk triggers for significant fire.  Hot, unstable, and potentially breezy 
conditions from thunderstorms could also develop over the lower Mississippi Valley. Over the western US, the 
main concern will revolve around very warm, dry and breezy condit ions forming in the afternoon and evening 
across portions of the Great Basin, northeast California and the Cascade gaps in eastern Oregon and 
Washington. The latter locations will see wind gusts of 30 -40 mph with RH values in the teens during the 
afternoon and early evening resulting elevated fire weather concerns. Over Alaska, most of the state will become 
cooler and wetter except for the Tanana Valley where precipitation isn’t expected at this time.  Elsewhere in the 
US, the main feature will be very hot temperatures over the Desert Southwest and a strong cold front sweeping 
southward from the northern Plains. The front will deliver cooling temperatures and scattered thunderstorms 
across the Upper Midwest to the western Great Lakes.
