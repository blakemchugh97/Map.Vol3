National Preparedness Level 5 
 
 
National Fire Activity: 
 
 
 
Understanding the IMSR 
 
IMSR Map 
 
Three 15-member incident management teams and 196 fireline management personnel from Australia and 
New Zealand are assigned to support large fires in the Northwest and Great Basin geographic areas.  
 
Two MAFFS C-130 airtankers from the 302nd Airlift Wing (Air Force Reserve, CO) and one from the 152nd 
Airlift Wing (NV Air National Guard), with respective support personnel, have been deployed to Klamath Falls, 
OR. Two MAFFS C-130 airtankers and support personnel from the 146th Airlift Wing (CA Air National Guard) 
have been deployed to Redding, CA to support wildland fire operations nationally. 
 
Two hundred forty soldiers from the 1st Battalion, 23rd Infantry Regiment, 7th Infantry Division based out of 
Joint Base Lewis McChord (JBLM) are deployed in support of wildland fire operations on the Modrite fire. An 
Incident Management Organization (IMO) is assigned to support the military contingent.

Predictive Services Discussion:  
 
Strong high pressure will drift westward into Desert Southwest today resulting in subtle warming and drying 
across much of the western US.  Warmer than normal temperatures are forecast for most of the West highs in 
the upper 80s and 90s across most of the West with readings in the 100s in the Interior Valley of California and 
into western Arizona.  This will combine with relative humidity values in the single digits and teens.  Winds will 
generally be less than 15 mph. The shifting ridge will also allow a c old front to drop into the Central Plains 
delivering numerous thunderstorms as well as easing the hot and dry conditions of late, especially across eastern 
Colorado, Kansas and Missouri. South of the front, across Texas, Oklahoma, and Arkansas the oppressive heat 
will continue with highs forecast in the 100s and relative humidity values in the teens. South winds gusts of 20 
to 25 mph in these areas will result in enhanced fire weather conditions.  The other area of concern will lie over 
southern Florida where scattered thunderstorms over record dry fuels could result in new fire starts and gusty 
outflow winds.
